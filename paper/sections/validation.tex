\subsection{Lid-driven cavity (pure fluid)}
The centreline velocity $u(y)$ at $x=0.5$ is compared to Ghia \emph{et al.}
\cite{Ghia1982}. The MAC solver attains an RMS error of $1.7\times10^{-3}$ at
$\mathrm{Re}=100$ and $2.8\times10^{-2}$ at $\mathrm{Re}=1000$ ($N=129$). The implicit
(IMEX) integrator reproduces the same steady state (RMS $1.68\times10^{-2}$ vs.\
explicit $1.71\times10^{-2}$ at $N=48$) and remains correct at $5\times$ the explicit
viscous CFL. \TODO{final high-resolution Ghia figure/table.}

\subsection{Soft disc in a lid-driven cavity (FSI)}
\label{sec:disc-lid}
A neo-Hookean disc carried by the cavity flow is compared to the full-Eulerian result of
Sugiyama \emph{et al.}\ \cite{Sugiyama2011} and to Jain \emph{et al.}\ \cite{Jain2019}.
The centroid trajectory matches the reference and the deformed interface is
grid-converged ($N=64\approx N=128$). The IMEX integrator reproduces the explicit
(validated) centroid trajectory to $2\times10^{-3}$ with identical minimum Jacobian,
confirming that the implicit viscosity does not perturb the validated FSI result
(\cref{fig:disc-centroid}, \cref{tab:disc-integrators}). Over the full inward spiral the
explicit, IMEX and monolithic integrators trace the same centroid path---with the
monolithic completing it in a quarter of the steps (\cref{sec:disc-speedup}).

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{figures/soft_disc_overlay.png}
\caption{Soft disc in a lid-driven cavity ($N{=}64$): centroid trajectory over the full
inward spiral for the explicit, IMEX and monolithic integrators, compared with Sugiyama
\emph{et al.}\ \cite{Sugiyama2011}. Explicit and IMEX are indistinguishable; the
monolithic (semi-Lagrangian, $4\times\dt$) traces the same spiral in ${\sim}4\times$ fewer

\subsection{Viscoelastic disc in a lid-driven cavity}
\label{sec:ve-fsi}
To validate the viscoelastic constitutive model \emph{in a two-way-coupled FSI setting}
(rather than only the $0$-D constitutive limits of \cref{sec:verification}), we replace the
neo-Hookean disc of \cref{sec:disc-lid} by a Maxwell-branch viscoelastic solid (modulus
$G$, relaxation time $\tau$), carrying $\psib=\log\be$ and using $\sig_{\!s}=G\,\dev(\be)$.
As $\tau\to\infty$ the elastic Finger tensor tracks $\Ften\Ften^{\!\top}$ and the model
reduces to the neo-Hookean solid; the coupled solver reproduces the neo-Hookean
centroid trajectory (itself validated against Sugiyama, \cref{sec:disc-lid}) to
$4.3\times10^{-3}$ over the shared time window (\cref{fig:ve-fsi}), so the viscoelastic FSI
coupling is anchored to a validated benchmark. For finite $\tau$ the relaxing branch lowers
the transient effective stiffness, so the disc deforms more than the elastic one and, at
$N{=}64$, reaches the diffuse-interface deformation limit earlier; long-time,
large-deformation robustness (via reinitialization/refinement) is left to future work.
The sharp viscoelastic signature is instead reported in the controlled extensional flow of
\cref{sec:results} (the analytic plateau $b_e^{xx}\to1/(1-2\mathrm{Wi})$).

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.6\linewidth]{figures/viscoelastic_fsi.png}
\caption{Viscoelastic disc in a lid-driven cavity ($N{=}64$): centroid trajectories for
$\tau=\infty$ (recovers the neo-Hookean solid), $\tau=2$ and $\tau=0.5$, with the
neo-Hookean reference (dashed). The $\tau=\infty$ curve overlays the neo-Hookean arc
(recovery to $4.3\times10^{-3}$); finite-$\tau$ discs deform more and terminate earlier at
this resolution.}
\label{fig:ve-fsi}
\end{figure}
