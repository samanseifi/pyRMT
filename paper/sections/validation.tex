\subsection{Lid-driven cavity (pure fluid)}
The centreline velocity $u(y)$ at $x=0.5$ is compared to Ghia \emph{et al.}
\cite{Ghia1982}. The MAC solver attains an RMS error of $1.7\times10^{-3}$ at
$\mathrm{Re}=100$ and $2.8\times10^{-2}$ at $\mathrm{Re}=1000$ ($N=129$). The implicit
(IMEX) integrator reproduces the same steady state (RMS $1.68\times10^{-2}$ vs.\
explicit $1.71\times10^{-2}$ at $N=48$) and remains correct at $5\times$ the explicit
viscous CFL. \TODO{final high-resolution Ghia figure/table.}

\subsection{Neo-Hookean (elastic) disc in a Taylor--Green vortex: FSI convergence}
\label{sec:disc-tg}
The first FSI test measures the spatial order of the coupled discretization. A
neo-Hookean \emph{elastic} disc is carried by a decaying Taylor--Green vortex---a smooth,
closed-streamline flow with no walls---so that the only error source is the one-fluid
interface coupling itself. Measuring the L2 error against the finest grid ($N{=}128$) over
$N=32$--$128$ (\cref{fig:disc-tg-conv}) gives velocity $\approx1.8$ and kinetic/total
energy $\approx2$, but \emph{pressure only $\approx1$}: the diffuse interface caps the
pressure (and, more mildly, the velocity) below the second order the same solver attains
on a wall-free single-mode fluid flow, consistent with Jain \emph{et al.}\
\cite{Jain2019}. The reduced order is intrinsic to the smoothed one-fluid interface, not
to the time integrator---the explicit, IMEX and semi-monolithic schemes share the spatial
discretization and coincide (\cref{sec:verification}); a sharp-interface treatment
\cite{Kolahdouz2023} is the route to higher field order and is a limitation of the present
method.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{figures/disc_tg_convergence.png}
\caption{Neo-Hookean disc in a Taylor--Green vortex: FSI spatial convergence of velocity,
pressure and kinetic/total energy (L2 vs the finest grid). Velocity is near second order
and the energy second order, while the pressure is limited to first order by the diffuse
one-fluid interface.}
\label{fig:disc-tg-conv}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Neo-Hookean (elastic) disc in a lid-driven cavity (FSI)}
\label{sec:disc-lid}
A neo-Hookean \emph{elastic} disc carried by the cavity flow is compared to the
full-Eulerian result of Sugiyama \emph{et al.}\ \cite{Sugiyama2011} and to Jain
\emph{et al.}\ \cite{Jain2019}.
The centroid trajectory follows the reference over the full inward spiral with a mean
deviation of $1.4\times10^{-2}$ at $N{=}64$ and $1.3\times10^{-2}$ at $N{=}128$
(max $\approx4\times10^{-2}$)---about $3.5\%$ of the trajectory extent. The deviation is
essentially grid-independent ($N{=}64\approx N{=}128$), so the residual gap to Sugiyama's
$1024^2$ result is a genuine diffuse-interface/model difference (\cref{sec:disc-tg}), not
under-resolution. The IMEX integrator reproduces the explicit
(validated) centroid trajectory to $2\times10^{-3}$ with identical minimum Jacobian,
confirming that the implicit viscosity does not perturb the validated FSI result
(\cref{fig:disc-centroid}, \cref{tab:disc-integrators}). Over the full inward spiral the
explicit, IMEX and semi-Lagrangian (IMEX-SL) integrators trace the same centroid
path---with IMEX-SL completing it in a quarter of the steps (\cref{sec:disc-speedup}).

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{figures/soft_disc_overlay.png}
\caption{Neo-Hookean disc in a lid-driven cavity ($N{=}64$): centroid trajectory over the
full inward spiral for the explicit, IMEX and IMEX-SL integrators, compared with Sugiyama
\emph{et al.}\ \cite{Sugiyama2011}. Explicit and IMEX are indistinguishable; the
semi-Lagrangian IMEX-SL ($4\times\dt$) traces the same spiral in ${\sim}4\times$ fewer
steps, with a small phase drift on the innermost turns.}
\label{fig:disc-centroid}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Viscoelastic constitutive response}
\label{sec:ve-fsi}
The finite-strain viscoelastic branch is validated against closed-form rheometric
solutions of the upper-convected Maxwell (UCM) model, exercising the log-conformation
update ($\psib=\log\be$, $\sig_{\!s}=G\be$) across transient, frequency-domain and
high-Weissenberg regimes (\cref{fig:ve-valid}; $G=\tau=1$ so $\mathrm{Wi}$ reads directly
off the imposed rate).
\emph{Startup of steady shear} (\cref{fig:ve-valid}a) reproduces the transient shear stress
$\sigma_{xy}(t)=G\tau\dot\gamma\,(1-e^{-t/\tau})$ and first normal-stress difference
$N_1(t)=2G\tau^2\dot\gamma^2\,[\,1-(1+t/\tau)e^{-t/\tau}]$ to a maximum error of
$1.8\times10^{-6}$.
\emph{Small-amplitude oscillatory shear} (\cref{fig:ve-valid}b) recovers the Maxwell
storage and loss moduli $G'(\omega)=G(\omega\tau)^2/[1+(\omega\tau)^2]$ and
$G''(\omega)=G\omega\tau/[1+(\omega\tau)^2]$---including the crossover at $\omega\tau=1$
and the elastic plateau $G'\!\to\!G$---to $2\times10^{-4}$ over three decades of frequency.
\emph{Steady planar extension} (\cref{fig:ve-valid}c) reproduces the analytic elastic
eigenvalues $b_e^{xx}=1/(1-2\mathrm{Wi})$ and $b_e^{yy}=1/(1+2\mathrm{Wi})$, tracking the
coil--stretch growth of $b_e^{xx}$ up to $\mathrm{Wi}\!\to\!\tfrac12$ (relative error below
$3\%$, set by the finite integration time near the singularity) while $\be$ remains
symmetric positive-definite throughout---the property the matrix-logarithm transport
guarantees and a direct $\be$ update loses. As $\tau\to\infty$ the branch reduces to the
neo-Hookean solid validated in \cref{sec:disc-lid}, to which the coupled FSI solver reduces
byte-for-byte; the two-way-coupled viscoelastic FSI at large, sustained deformation
(where reinitialization/refinement become necessary) is left to future work.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/viscoelastic_validation.png}
\caption{Viscoelastic constitutive validation of the log-conformation update against
analytic UCM/Maxwell solutions ($G=\tau=1$): (a) startup of steady shear at
$\mathrm{Wi}=1$---transient $\sigma_{xy}$ and $N_1$ (markers) vs analytic (grey);
(b) SAOS storage/loss moduli $G',G''$ vs Maxwell over three decades of $\omega\tau$;
(c) steady planar-extension eigenvalues of $\be$ vs $1/(1\mp2\mathrm{Wi})$, showing the
coil--stretch approach to $\mathrm{Wi}=\tfrac12$ (dotted). Maximum errors
$1.8\times10^{-6}$, $2\times10^{-4}$ and ${<}3\%$ respectively.}
\label{fig:ve-valid}
\end{figure}
