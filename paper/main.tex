\title{An implicit, monolithic, incompressible Reference Map Technique for
viscoelastic fluid--structure interaction}

\author[a]{Saman Seifi\corref{cor}}
\cortext[cor]{Corresponding author}
\affiliation[a]{organization={Affiliation TODO}, country={Country TODO}}

\begin{abstract}
We present a fully Eulerian, one-fluid Reference Map Technique (RMT) for
incompressible fluid--structure interaction (FSI) that couples a finite-strain
viscoelastic solid to a Newtonian fluid on a single collocated velocity field and a
single pressure solve. Two contributions distinguish the method from the explicit,
compressible, or fluid-only formulations in the literature. First, the stress-carrying
strain is transported as a log-conformation tensor $\psib=\log\be$ (Fattal--Kupferman),
which is positive-definite by construction and decouples the relaxing elastic stress
from the folding-prone reference map. Second, the time discretization is made
\emph{implicit and monolithic}: the stiff viscous, relaxation and elastic-wave terms
are advanced without the explicit CFL restrictions through an implicit--explicit (IMEX)
stack that reuses the pressure transform/Krylov solvers, culminating in a semi-Lagrangian, preconditioned, implicit scheme that removes
\emph{every} explicit CFL restriction (viscous, relaxation, elastic-wave and advective)
on the coupled velocity--pressure--reference-map--conformation system. The staggered
(MAC) discretization makes the projection exact (machine-zero divergence). We verify the
constitutive update against analytic viscometric limits to machine precision, confirm the
exact projection and spatial order on manufactured and Taylor--Green problems, and
validate the coupled solver against the Ghia lid-driven cavity and the Sugiyama soft-disc
benchmark. The implicit elastic coupling is energy-conserving (no numerical damping), and
a DST-preconditioned solve keeps the per-step cost at a few iterations independent of
resolution, so the lifted time step becomes a wall-clock gain: on the soft-disc benchmark
the scheme reaches $6\times$ the explicit time step for a $3\times$ net speedup while
reproducing the trajectory, and it reaches high-Weissenberg regimes inaccessible to
explicit RMT.
\end{abstract}
