\title{A semi-implicit log-conformation Reference Map Technique for incompressible
viscoelastic fluid--structure interaction}

\author[bu]{Saman Seifi\corref{cor}}\ead{samansei@bu.edu}
\cortext[cor]{Corresponding author}
\address[bu]{Department of Mechanical Engineering, Boston University,
Boston, MA 02215, USA}

\begin{abstract}
We present a fully Eulerian, one-fluid Reference Map Technique (RMT) for incompressible
fluid--structure interaction (FSI) that couples a finite-strain \emph{viscoelastic} solid
to a Newtonian fluid on a single staggered (MAC) grid with one pressure solve. The
stress-carrying elastic strain is transported as a log-conformation tensor
$\psib=\log\be$, positive-definite by construction, which decouples the relaxing elastic
stress from the folding-prone reference map; as the relaxation time $\tau\to\infty$ the
model reduces to the neo-Hookean RMT. On this spatial method we build a family of four
time-integration schemes of increasing implicitness---explicit; an implicit--explicit
(IMEX) scheme with backward-Euler viscosity through the pressure transforms and an exact
exponential relaxation; a semi-Lagrangian variant (IMEX-SL); and a semi-monolithic
Newton--Krylov (JFNK) solve of the velocity--pressure system---which remove the viscous,
relaxation, elastic-wave and advective step-size limits in turn, each verified to reproduce
the explicit result before its target limit is lifted. The coupled elastic and viscoelastic
FSI runs use the IMEX-SL scheme with an energy-conserving trapezoidal elastic coupling; the
semi-monolithic Newton--Krylov solve, which makes advection implicit and consistent, is
demonstrated here on the fluid, and the fully-coupled Newton over $(\uv,p,\xiv,\psib)$ is
posed and left as the next step. The staggered discretization makes the projection exact
(machine-zero divergence), and the trapezoidal coupling conserves the discrete energy to
round-off at every step. We validate the constitutive update against analytic Maxwell
solutions (startup of shear, oscillatory moduli, planar extension), the fluid and
neo-Hookean-solid FSI against the lid-driven cavity and a disc-in-vortex convergence study,
and a two-way-coupled \emph{viscoelastic} (standard-linear-solid) disc in a Taylor--Green
vortex, where the relaxing branch dissipates stored elastic energy, the equilibrium branch
retains shape, and $\be$ stays positive-definite. A work--precision study delineates where
the implicit stack pays off: the semi-Lagrangian scheme reaches a target accuracy at lower
wall-clock by taking several-fold larger steps, while the benefit of the implicit
viscous/elastic solves is set by how far the step can grow relative to the per-step solve
cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Fluid--structure interaction (FSI) problems in which a soft solid undergoes large
deformation inside a flow---valves, swimmers, capsules, gels and polymeric
particles---are numerically demanding because the solid and fluid share a moving
interface across which stress and velocity must remain continuous. The dominant
strategies treat the two phases with different kinematics. Arbitrary
Lagrangian--Eulerian (ALE) methods deform a body-fitted mesh with the interface
\cite{Donea1982,Hu2001}, which is accurate but requires remeshing once the
deformation becomes severe. Immersed and fictitious-domain methods keep the fluid
grid fixed and represent the solid through forcing or Lagrange multipliers
\cite{Peskin2002,Zhang2004}, avoiding remeshing at the cost of interface sharpness.
Partitioned couplings that iterate between separate fluid and solid solvers are
modular but suffer the added-mass instability when the densities are comparable, so
that strong coupling or careful relaxation is required for stability
\cite{Causin2005,Hou2012}. These difficulties have motivated a third route: fully
\emph{Eulerian}, one-fluid formulations that discretize solid and fluid on a single
fixed grid, sharing one velocity field and one pressure solve
\cite{Sugiyama2011,Ii2012,Cottet2008,Wick2013}. The interface is captured rather than
tracked, the added-mass coupling is resolved monolithically inside the momentum
balance, and no remeshing is ever needed; the price is that the finite-strain solid
response, naturally posed on a material reference, must be reconstructed in the
laboratory frame.

The \emph{reference map technique} (RMT) supplies exactly this reconstruction. It
transports the inverse motion---the reference map $\xiv(\xv,t)$ carrying a current
point back to its material label---as an Eulerian field, from which the deformation
gradient $\Ften=(\grad\xiv)^{-1}$ and hence the elastic stress follow pointwise
\cite{Kamrin2012,Valkov2015}. Since its introduction the RMT has grown into a robust
incompressible FSI solver with level-set interface capture and stress blending
\cite{Rycroft2020}, and into a conservative, non-dissipative finite-difference
formulation \cite{Jain2019}; it belongs to the same Eulerian-solid lineage as the
full-Eulerian schemes of Sugiyama \emph{et al.}\ \cite{Sugiyama2011,Sugiyama2010} and
the earlier continuum description of viscoelastic particles by Liu and Walkington
\cite{Liu2001}. The appeal is a single set of conservation laws on one staggered
grid; the open questions concern \emph{what} constitutive branch the solid carries and
\emph{how} the coupled system is advanced in time.

Both questions are consequential. On the constitutive side, the established RMT
formulations carry a purely \emph{hyperelastic} solid \cite{Kamrin2012,Rycroft2020,Jain2019}.
A hyperelastic body sheared without bound stores unbounded energy, and its Eulerian
carrier---the reference map, or equivalently the elastic strain---folds under sustained
shear, so that reinitialization or filtering is used to treat the symptom. A \emph{relaxing}
stress helps, but it is important to state what it does and does not do: relaxation bounds
the \emph{stored elastic strain} and makes the stress insensitive to a folding reference
map---the stress is then carried by the bounded, always-positive-definite $\be$ rather than
by $\Ften=(\grad\xiv)^{-1}$---but it does not prevent the map itself from folding, and a
more fluid-like (smaller-$\tau$) body in fact stretches and folds sooner. Relaxation thus
relocates the folding difficulty out of the stress; only in the Maxwell-fluid limit, where
the map need not be differentiated at all, is it removed entirely. Transporting a
viscoelastic stress stably is itself a classical difficulty---the high-Weissenberg
number problem, in which a naive update of the conformation tensor loses
positive-definiteness and the computation breaks down \cite{Oldroyd1950,Bird1987}.
The cure is to evolve a matrix logarithm of the conformation tensor, the
log-conformation representation of Fattal and Kupferman \cite{Fattal2004,Fattal2005},
subsequently recast in eigenframe form by Hulsen \emph{et al.}\ \cite{Hulsen2005} and
placed in a broader factorization framework by Balci \emph{et al.}\ \cite{Balci2011},
which guarantees a symmetric positive-definite conformation by construction. Bringing
this representation into the RMT ties the relaxing elastic strain of the solid to the
same stable transport that viscoelastic \emph{fluids} already enjoy, and decouples it
from the folding-prone reference map.

On the time-integration side, the same formulations advance momentum explicitly, so
the step is throttled simultaneously by the elastic-wave speed $c_s=\sqrt{\mu_s/\rho}$,
the viscous diffusion $\dx^2/\nu$, the advective CFL, and---once the solid relaxes---the
relaxation time $\tau$. Stiff solids and high-Weissenberg regimes are then effectively
out of reach. Implicit and monolithic Eulerian FSI does exist, but for
\emph{hyperelastic} solids: Dunne \cite{Dunne2006}, Richter \cite{Richter2013,Richter2017}
and Wick \cite{Wick2013} solve a coupled Newton system for velocity, pressure and a
solid deformation field on a fixed grid, tracking the interface with an ``initial point
set'' that is itself a reference-map-like field. Even there the deformation/interface is
advanced through a separate extension step outside the Jacobian, a treatment that is not
\emph{fully} implicit in the geometry. Sharp-interface implicit FSI for large-deformation
solids has likewise been developed by Kolahdouz \emph{et al.}\ \cite{Kolahdouz2023}, but
through a \emph{partitioned} Dirichlet--Neumann coupling on a separate Lagrangian solid
mesh rather than a monolithic Eulerian solve. In none of these is a finite-strain
\emph{viscoelastic} solid coupled to a fluid and advanced implicitly on a single grid.

This paper develops that method. Its contribution is the numerical formulation itself:
a semi-implicit, log-conformation Reference Map Technique for incompressible
viscoelastic FSI on a marker-and-cell (MAC) staggered grid, together with a family of
time integrators that remove the stiff step-size limits one at a time. The solid stress
is carried by a log-conformation tensor $\psib=\log\be$, positive-definite by
construction and, in the standard-linear-solid form, retaining an equilibrium
hyperelastic branch so the body still returns to shape while $\tau\!\to\!\infty$
recovers the neo-Hookean RMT exactly. The staggered discretization makes the pressure
projection exact---divergence is driven to machine zero---removing the
checkerboard and Rhie--Chow workarounds that a collocated arrangement requires
\cite{Harlow1965,Chorin1968,Brown2001}. On top of this spatial method we build four
time-integration schemes of increasing implicitness. An explicit scheme reproduces the
established RMT and fixes the reference. An implicit--explicit (IMEX) scheme treats the
viscous term by backward Euler through the pressure transforms and Krylov solvers, the
relaxation by an exact exponential update, and the elastic coupling by an
energy-conserving trapezoidal rule, lifting the viscous, relaxation and elastic-wave
limits in a fractional-step setting. A semi-Lagrangian variant additionally removes the
advective CFL restriction and gives the coupled elastic and viscoelastic FSI results.
Finally, a Jacobian-free Newton--Krylov (JFNK) scheme solves the velocity--pressure system
\emph{semi-monolithically}, making advection implicit and consistent (removing the
operator-split error), preconditioned by one step of the split IMEX solver with the exact
projection acting as the pressure Schur complement; we demonstrate it on the fluid, with the
reference map and conformation held fixed within the Newton iteration. Each scheme is
verified to reproduce the explicit result before its target limit is lifted, and we report a
work--precision comparison that delineates where implicit integration pays for itself. The
consistent linearized neo-Hookean tangent underlying the implicit coupling, and the
physics-based preconditioning that makes the Newton solve tractable, follow established
practice for stiff nonlinear systems
\cite{Knoll2004,Kelley1995,Ascher1997,Reese1998,Simo1998}.

We are deliberate about scope. The IMEX and semi-Lagrangian schemes are fully implemented
and validated, and produce all the coupled elastic and viscoelastic FSI results; the
semi-monolithic Newton--Krylov scheme is realized and demonstrated for the fluid
velocity--pressure system, and closing it to the fully-coupled $(\uv,p,\xiv,\psib)$ Newton is
identified as the remaining step. Every capability is reported at its true status.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
\Cref{sec:governing,sec:rmt,sec:constitutive} set out the continuous one-fluid model,
the reference map, and the finite-strain viscoelastic constitutive law;
\cref{sec:discretization,sec:time} give the MAC discretization and the four
time-integration schemes with their algorithms;
and \cref{sec:verification,sec:validation,sec:results} present the verification against
analytic and manufactured limits, the FSI validation against the disc-in-vortex and
lid-driven-cavity benchmarks, and the work--precision comparison.

\section{Governing equations}
\label{sec:governing}

We consider an incompressible medium on a fixed domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^2$
occupied by a Newtonian fluid and one or more finite-strain solid bodies. A single
velocity field $\uv(\xv,t)$ and pressure $p(\xv,t)$ are shared by both phases. Momentum
and incompressibility read
\begin{align}
\rho\left(\partial_t\uv + (\uv\!\cdot\!\grad)\uv\right)
   &= -\grad p + \dive\sig_{\mathrm{d}} + \bm f_{\Gamma},
   \label{eq:mom}\\
\dive\uv &= 0,
   \label{eq:incomp}
\end{align}
where $\rho$ is the (piecewise-constant) density and $\bm f_\Gamma$ is the surface-tension
force (a penalty contact force for solid--solid near-contact is available in the framework
but is not exercised by any case reported here). The deviatoric stress $\sig_{\mathrm d}$ is
blended between fluid and solid through a smoothed indicator (one-fluid model),
\begin{equation}
\sig_{\mathrm d} = 2\mu_f\,\bm D
   + (1-H)\,\sig_{\!s},
   \qquad
\bm D=\tfrac12\!\left(\grad\uv+\grad\uv^{\!\top}\right),
   \label{eq:blend}
\end{equation}
with $\mu_f$ the fluid viscosity, $\bm D$ the rate-of-strain, $\sig_{\!s}$ the solid
Cauchy stress (\cref{sec:rmt,sec:constitutive}), and $H\in[0,1]$ a smoothed Heaviside
of the level set $\phi$ ($H\to1$ in the fluid, $H\to0$ in the solid). The trace of
$\sig_{\!s}$ is carried by the pressure, so only its deviatoric part enters
\cref{eq:mom} up to a redefinition of $p$.

The material occupied by the solid is tracked by the reference map (\cref{sec:rmt}),
from which the level set $\phi$ and the deformation are reconstructed. Boundary
conditions are problem-dependent (no-slip walls with an optional moving lid, free-slip
impermeable walls, or periodic); the pressure boundary condition is chosen consistently
with the velocity boundary condition (\cref{sec:discretization}).

\section{The reference map and the elastic solid}
\label{sec:rmt}

\subsection{Reference map and deformation gradient}
Let $\bm\chi(\Xv,t)$ be the motion carrying a material label $\Xv$ to its current
position $\xv$. The \emph{reference map} is the inverse,
$\xiv(\xv,t)=\bm\chi^{-1}(\xv,t)$, so $\xiv(\xv,0)=\xv$ in the solid. Because $\xiv$
labels material, it is advected passively,
\begin{equation}
\partial_t\xiv + (\uv\!\cdot\!\grad)\xiv = \bm 0 .
\label{eq:xi-advect}
\end{equation}
The deformation gradient $\Ften=\partial\xv/\partial\Xv$ is recovered in the Eulerian
frame from the spatial gradient of the reference map,
\begin{equation}
\Ften = (\grad\xiv)^{-1},
\qquad J=\det\Ften ,
\label{eq:F}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Neo-Hookean solid}
The equilibrium (non-relaxing) branch of the solid is an incompressible neo-Hookean
material,
\begin{equation}
\sig_{\!s}^{\infty} = \mu_s\,\dev\!\left(\Ften\Ften^{\!\top}\right)
   = \mu_s\!\left(\bt - \tfrac12\tr(\bt)\,\It\right)\quad(\text{2-D}),
\label{eq:neohookean}
\end{equation}
with shear modulus $\mu_s$ and elastic wave speed $c_s=\sqrt{\mu_s/\rho}$. In the purely
hyperelastic limit ($\tau\to\infty$, \cref{sec:constitutive}) this is the entire solid
stress; the general viscoelastic solid adds a relaxing branch.

\subsection{Level set, extrapolation, reinitialization}
The solid occupies $\{\phi\le0\}$. The level set is reconstructed from the reference map
each step (the material boundary is the image of the initial boundary under $\xiv$), and
the reference map is extrapolated into a narrow band outside the solid so that the
gradient in \cref{eq:F} and the interfacial forces have valid stencils. Optional
level-set reinitialization (PDE or fast-marching) restores a signed-distance band. The
advection of \cref{eq:xi-advect} is discretized by a switchable scheme (semi-Lagrangian
RK4 backtrace by default; central/WENO or a conservative flux form for
differentiability, \cref{sec:time}).

\section{Finite-strain viscoelasticity via log-conformation}
\label{sec:constitutive}

\subsection{Standard-linear-solid model}
The solid stress is split into a non-relaxing (equilibrium) neo-Hookean branch
\cref{eq:neohookean} and a relaxing Maxwell branch carried by an elastic Finger tensor
$\be$,
\begin{equation}
\sig_{\!s} = \mu_s\,\dev\!\left(\Ften\Ften^{\!\top}\right)
   + G\,\dev(\be),
\label{eq:sls}
\end{equation}
where $G$ is the viscoelastic modulus. The elastic Finger tensor evolves by the
upper-convected Maxwell (UCM) equation with relaxation time $\tau$,
\begin{equation}
\Dt{\be} - \Lt\be - \be\Lt^{\!\top} = -\frac1\tau\left(\be-\It\right),
\qquad \Lt=\grad\uv,
\label{eq:ucm}
\end{equation}
with $\tfrac{D}{Dt}=\partial_t+(\uv\!\cdot\!\grad)$. Dropping the equilibrium branch
($\mu_s=0$) gives a Maxwell fluid; $\tau\to\infty$ gives a purely elastic solid for
which \cref{eq:ucm} reduces to $\Dt{\be}=\Lt\be+\be\Lt^{\!\top}$, i.e.\ $\be$ tracks
$\Ften\Ften^{\!\top}$ and \cref{eq:sls} returns the neo-Hookean stress. The model
therefore spans, under one framework, elastic solid $\leftrightarrow$ viscoelastic
solid $\leftrightarrow$ viscoelastic fluid.

The model derives from the free energy
$\Psi=\tfrac{\mu_s}{2}\!\left(\tr\Ften\Ften^{\!\top}-2\right)
      +\tfrac{G}{2}\!\left(\tr\be-2-\ln\det\be\right)$,
with the relaxation term giving a non-negative dissipation $\tfrac{G}{2\tau}\,
\tr(\be+\be^{-1}-2\It)\ge0$; \cref{eq:sls} is its (deviatoric) Cauchy stress.
Two honest caveats. \emph{(i) Folding.} Only the relaxing branch is bounded by
relaxation; the equilibrium branch $\mu_s\dev(\Ften\Ften^{\!\top})$ is still built from
$\Ften=(\grad\xiv)^{-1}$ and can fold under unbounded shear, so relaxation eliminates the
folding sensitivity only in the Maxwell-fluid limit and mitigates it otherwise.
\emph{(ii) Incompressibility of $\be$.} The relaxation toward $\It$ does not preserve
$\det\be=1$ (as in Oldroyd-B); the trace is carried by the pressure, and $\det\be$ is
monitored as a diagnostic.

\paragraph{Viscometric limits (used for verification).} For homogeneous simple shear
$\Lt=\dot\gamma\,\bm e_x\!\otimes\!\bm e_y$ the steady solution of \cref{eq:ucm} is
$b_e^{xy}=\tau\dot\gamma$, $b_e^{xx}=1+2\tau^2\dot\gamma^2$, $b_e^{yy}=1$, giving
$\sigma_{xy}=G\tau\dot\gamma$ and first normal-stress difference
$N_1=2G\tau^2\dot\gamma^2$. For a step shear strain $\gamma$ held from $t=0$,
$b_e^{xy}(t)=\gamma\,e^{-t/\tau}$; for uniform planar extension at rate $\varepsilon$
with $\mathrm{Wi}=\varepsilon\tau<\tfrac12$, $b_e^{xx}\to1/(1-2\mathrm{Wi})$.

\subsection{Log-conformation transport}
Direct transport of $\be$ is either over-diffusive or, if non-dissipative, loses
positive-definiteness and blows up at high Weissenberg number. Following
Fattal--Kupferman \cite{Fattal2004} we evolve
\begin{equation}
\psib=\log\be, \qquad \be=\exp\psib\succ0\ \text{by construction.}
\label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
Advection of $\psib$ is passive as in \cref{eq:xi-advect}; the local (homogeneous) part
of \cref{eq:ucm} is transformed to $\psib$ as follows, in the eigenframe form of Hulsen
\emph{et al.}\ \cite{Hulsen2005} (equivalent to, but distinct from, the
$\bm\Omega+\bm B+\bm N$ decomposition of \cite{Fattal2004}). In the eigenframe of $\psib$
(equivalently of $\be$), with eigenvalues $\lambda_i=e^{\ell_i}$ of $\be$ and
$\bm M=\bm R^{\!\top}\Lt\bm R$ the velocity gradient rotated into that frame, the local
evolution of $\psib$ is
\begin{equation}
\dot{\psib}\big|_{\mathrm{eig}}
   = \begin{bmatrix} 2M_{11} + \tfrac1\tau(e^{-\ell_1}-1) & \Omega_{12}\\[2pt]
       \Omega_{12} & 2M_{22} + \tfrac1\tau(e^{-\ell_2}-1)\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\Omega_{12}=\frac{\ell_2-\ell_1}{\lambda_2-\lambda_1}\left(\lambda_2 M_{12}+\lambda_1 M_{21}\right),
\label{eq:logconf-rhs}
\end{equation}
rotated back by $\bm R(\cdot)\bm R^{\!\top}$; the diagonal carries stretching and
relaxation, the off-diagonal the eigenvector rotation. The degenerate limit
$\ell_1\!\to\!\ell_2$ of $\Omega_{12}$ is $e^{-\ell_1}(M_{12}+M_{21})$, recovering
$\dot{\psib}=2\,\mathrm{sym}(\Lt)$ at $\be=\It$, so \cref{eq:logconf-rhs} is smooth.
In two dimensions the eigendecomposition of the symmetric $2\times2$ field is available
in closed form, so \cref{eq:logconf-rhs} is evaluated without iterative diagonalization.

\subsection{Exact relaxation for stiff \texorpdfstring{$\tau$}{tau}}
\label{sec:exact-relax}
The relaxation term $-\tfrac1\tau(\be-\It)$ is stiff for small $\tau$ (high
Weissenberg), forcing $\dt\lesssim\tau$ under explicit integration. We remove this by
Strang-splitting the local update into
$\text{relax}(\dt/2)\!\to\!\text{stretch}(\dt)\!\to\!\text{relax}(\dt/2)$ and
integrating the relaxation substep \emph{analytically}. The relaxation-only equation
$\dot{\be}=-\tfrac1\tau(\be-\It)$ has the exact solution
\begin{equation}
\be \leftarrow \It + (\be-\It)\,e^{-\dt/\tau},
\label{eq:relax-exact}
\end{equation}
which is unconditionally stable for any $\tau$ and positive-definite-preserving, since
\cref{eq:relax-exact} is the convex combination $(1-e^{-\dt/\tau})\It+e^{-\dt/\tau}\be$
of two SPD tensors. The stretch substep uses \cref{eq:logconf-rhs} with $\tau\to\infty$
(explicit, non-stiff, bounded by the velocity-gradient magnitude). For a step strain
this scheme is exact at any $\dt$; the property is verified in \cref{sec:verification}.

\section{Spatial discretization and exact projection}
\label{sec:discretization}

\subsection{Staggered (MAC) grid}
The domain $[0,L_x]\times[0,L_y]$ is divided into $N_x\times N_y$ cells of size
$\dx\times\Delta y$. Pressure and the reference map live at cell centres,
$x$-velocity $u$ on the $x$-faces, and $y$-velocity $v$ on the $y$-faces
(Marker-and-Cell layout \cite{Harlow1965}). The discrete divergence (to centres),
pressure gradient (to faces) and Laplacian ($\dive\grad$) are then \emph{consistent by
construction}, $\mathrm{div}=-\mathrm{grad}^{\!\top}$ and
$\mathrm{Lap}=\mathrm{div}\circ\mathrm{grad}$, so the projection is exact.

\subsection{Fractional-step projection}
Momentum is advanced by a predictor--projector (Chorin) split \cite{Chorin1968}. Given a
predictor velocity $\uv^{*}$, the projection solves a pressure-Poisson problem and
corrects to a divergence-free field,
\begin{equation}
\nabla^2\phi = \frac{\rho}{\dt}\,\dive\uv^{*},
\qquad
\uv^{n+1}=\uv^{*}-\frac{\dt}{\rho}\,\grad\phi,
\qquad
\dive\uv^{n+1}=0\ \text{(to machine precision).}
\label{eq:project}
\end{equation}
On the staggered grid the correction is exact: the projected velocity is
divergence-free to round-off, without the checkerboard mode or Rhie--Chow interpolation
required by a collocated arrangement.

\subsection{Direct Poisson solvers}
The pressure boundary condition is chosen consistently with the velocity boundary
condition. For no-slip/free-slip walls the homogeneous-Neumann Laplacian is diagonalized
by the discrete cosine transform (DCT), with eigenvalues
$\lambda_{k}=-2\!\left(1-\cos(\pi k/N)\right)/\dx^2$; for periodic problems the FFT is
used, $\lambda_{k}=-4\sin^2(\pi k/N)/\dx^2$. Each solve is $O(N\log N)$. These
transforms are reused verbatim by the implicit viscous solve of \cref{sec:time}.

\subsection{Blended forcing}
The solid stress \cref{eq:sls} is blended by $(1-H)$, and its divergence is added to the
face momentum. Surface tension is a balanced-force continuum-surface-force model
$\bm f_\Gamma=-\gamma\kappa\,\grad H$ with $\kappa=\dive(\grad\phi/|\grad\phi|)$, in
which $\grad H$ uses the same compact face gradient as the pressure, so a constant-%
curvature interface is discretely balanceable by pressure (small parasitic currents)
\cite{Brackbill1992}. Contact between solids is applied as a trace-free stress whose
divergence survives the projection \cite{Rycroft2020}.

\section{Time integration: from explicit to implicit--monolithic}
\label{sec:time}

\subsection{Explicit baseline and its CFL restrictions}
One step of the explicit scheme advects $(\xiv,\psib)$ with the cell-centred velocity,
extrapolates into the band, assembles the blended stress \cref{eq:sls} and forces, takes
a forward-Euler momentum predictor, and projects \cref{eq:project}. Stability requires
\begin{equation}
\dt \le \min\!\Big(
\underbrace{C\,\dx/U}_{\text{advection}},\;
\underbrace{C_\nu\,\dx^2/\nu}_{\text{viscous}},\;
\underbrace{C\,\dx/c_s}_{\text{elastic wave}}\Big),
\qquad \text{and}\quad \dt\lesssim\tau,
\label{eq:cfl}
\end{equation}
with $c_s=\sqrt{\mu_s/\rho}$. The viscous, relaxation and elastic-wave limits are
progressively removed below; advection is retained explicitly because the
semi-Lagrangian backtrace is already unconditionally stable.

\subsection{IMEX stack}
The strategies form a stack, each making one more stiff term of \cref{eq:cfl} implicit,
selected by a single keyword (\texttt{explicit} $\subset$ \texttt{imex} $\subset$
\texttt{imex-elastic}, with a \texttt{-sl} suffix adding semi-Lagrangian advection).
Levels (i)--(iii) below lift the viscous, relaxation and elastic-wave limits; the
semi-Lagrangian advection of \cref{sec:monolithic} lifts the remaining advective limit.

\paragraph{(i) Implicit viscosity.} The viscous term is advanced by backward Euler,
yielding a Helmholtz solve for the predictor,
\begin{equation}
\left(\It-\dt\,\nu\nabla^2\right)\uv^{*}
   = \uv^{n} - \dt\,(\uv\!\cdot\!\grad)\uv^{n} + \dt\,\bm f/\rho .
\label{eq:helmholtz}
\end{equation}
On periodic and Neumann directions the Helmholtz operator is diagonalized by the
\emph{same} transform (FFT/DCT) as the pressure Poisson solve of
\cref{sec:discretization}: in spectral space each mode is divided by
$1-\dt\,\nu\,\lambda_k>0$, so \cref{eq:helmholtz} is unconditionally stable and costs one
extra transform per component. For Dirichlet (no-slip $+$ moving lid) walls, where the
DCT does not diagonalize the operator, \cref{eq:helmholtz} is solved matrix-free by
conjugate gradients on the same ghost-cell viscous stencil (symmetric positive-definite,
hence CG-convergent), with the moving lid entering as a right-hand-side inhomogeneity;
this route also accommodates the variable one-fluid viscosity.

\paragraph{(ii) Exact relaxation.} The stiff relaxation of \cref{eq:ucm} is removed by
the analytic Strang substep \cref{eq:relax-exact}, which is $A$-stable in $\tau$ and
positive-definite-preserving (\cref{sec:exact-relax}).

\paragraph{(iii) Linearly-implicit elastic-wave stabilizer.}
\label{sec:elastic-stab}
The nonlinear elastic force $\dive\sig_{\!s}$ is kept explicit (physics), while an
$O(\dt^2)$ wave operator is added implicitly to \cref{eq:helmholtz},
\begin{equation}
\left(\It-\dt\,\nu\nabla^2-\dt^2 c_s^2\,\chi\,\nabla^2\right)\uv^{*}
   = \uv^{n}-\dt\,(\uv\!\cdot\!\grad)\uv^{n}+\dt\,\bm f/\rho,
\label{eq:elastic-stab}
\end{equation}
with $\chi=1-H$ the solid indicator. A frozen-coefficient split (constant $c_s^2$
treated implicitly everywhere, removed from the fluid by an explicit defect) keeps a
single transform solve. Crucially the elastic force must sit \emph{inside} the implicit
right-hand side, as the following stability analysis shows.

\begin{proposition}[Unconditional stability of the elastic stabilizer]
\label{prop:vn}
Consider the linearized elastic wave $\uv_t=c_s^2 d_{xx},\ d_t=\uv$ ($d$ the
displacement), discretized as
$\uv^{n+1}=\uv^{n}+\dt\,c_s^2 d^{n}_{xx}+\dt^2 c_s^2\,\uv^{n+1}_{xx}$,
$d^{n+1}=d^{n}+\dt\,\uv^{n+1}$. For a Fourier mode $k$ with $a\equiv\dt^2 c_s^2 k^2\ge0$
the amplification matrix has spectral radius $|\lambda|=1/\sqrt{1+a}\le1$; the scheme is
unconditionally stable, with mild damping that vanishes as $\dt\to0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With $\partial_{xx}\to-k^2$ the update is
$(1+a)\uv^{n+1}=\uv^n-\dt c_s^2 k^2 d^n$, $d^{n+1}=d^n+\dt\,\uv^{n+1}$. Eliminating
gives the map $\bm M=\tfrac{1}{1+a}\begin{psmallmatrix}1 & -\dt c_s^2 k^2\\ \dt & 1\end{psmallmatrix}$
with $\det\bm M=1/(1+a)$ and $\operatorname{tr}\bm M=2/(1+a)$. The eigenvalues solve
$\lambda^2-\tfrac{2}{1+a}\lambda+\tfrac1{1+a}=0$, i.e.\
$\lambda=(1\pm i\sqrt a)/(1+a)$, so $|\lambda|^2=(1+a)/(1+a)^2=1/(1+a)\le1$. If instead
the force is applied \emph{after} the implicit solve, the analogous map has
$\det=1/(1+a)$ but $\operatorname{tr}=1/(1+a)+1-a$, whose eigenvalues exceed unity for
$a>1$ (unstable). \hfill
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Interpretation and the role of the ``inside'' force]
\label{rem:vn}
The scheme is non-oscillatory and stable for every $\dt$ and wavenumber $k$: resolved long
waves ($a\ll1$) lose only $O(\dt^2)$ energy per step, vanishing under refinement, while
grid-scale waves ($a\gg1$) are strongly damped, supplying the dissipation that lets an
explicit force be stabilized. The proof also shows why the force must sit \emph{inside} the
implicit solve: applying it afterwards leaves the eigenvalue product unchanged but raises
their sum, forcing $|\lambda|>1$ once $a>1$---an absolute instability that only the
``inside'' form, advancing displacement and velocity with the same implicit force, avoids.
\end{remark}

The stage-1 damping is the joint price of the isotropic-Laplacian stabilizer and a
one-sided (backward-Euler-type) coupling; symmetrizing the time coupling removes it
completely, which is the scheme actually used for the elastic branch.

\begin{proposition}[Energy-conserving trapezoidal coupling]
\label{prop:trap}
Advance the same linear elastic wave by the trapezoidal (implicit-midpoint) rule,
\begin{equation*}
\uv^{n+1}=\uv^{n}+\tfrac{\dt}{2}\,c_s^2\,(d^n+d^{n+1})_{xx},\qquad
d^{n+1}=d^{n}+\tfrac{\dt}{2}\,(\uv^{n}+\uv^{n+1}).
\end{equation*}
Then $|\lambda|=1$ for every Fourier mode and every $\dt$: the scheme is unconditionally
stable and \emph{lossless}, exactly conserving the discrete energy
$\tfrac12(\uv^2+c_s^2 d_x^2)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Writing $\bm w=(\uv,d)$, the semi-discrete wave is $\dot{\bm w}=\bm A\bm w$ with
$\bm A=\begin{psmallmatrix}0 & -c_s^2 k^2\\ 1 & 0\end{psmallmatrix}$ (using
$\partial_{xx}\to-k^2$), whose eigenvalues $\pm i c_s k$ are purely imaginary. The
trapezoidal rule is the Cayley transform
$\bm w^{n+1}=(\bm I-\tfrac{\dt}{2}\bm A)^{-1}(\bm I+\tfrac{\dt}{2}\bm A)\,\bm w^{n}$,
whose eigenvalues $(1+\tfrac{\dt}{2}(\pm i c_s k))/(1-\tfrac{\dt}{2}(\pm i c_s k))$ have
unit modulus; the Cayley transform of a Hamiltonian matrix is symplectic, so the discrete
energy is conserved. \hfill
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Stage-1 vs.\ trapezoidal]
Proposition~\ref{prop:vn} gives a dissipative, extremely robust integrator (useful as a
stabilizer or for strongly stiff transients); Proposition~\ref{prop:trap} gives a
neutrally-stable, energy-conserving one. The solver uses the trapezoidal form for the
elastic branch so that a persistently oscillating solid neither grows nor decays
numerically; \cref{sec:verification} verifies the predicted energy conservation on a
standing elastic shear wave.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Toward a fully-coupled Newton solve}
\label{sec:monolithic}
The end point of the stack would solve the coupled system implicitly for the whole state
$\bm X=(\uv,p,\xiv,\psib)^{n+1}$. The backward-Euler residual is
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:residual}
\begin{align}
\bm R_{\!\uv} &= \rho\,\frac{\uv-\tilde{\uv}}{\dt} + \grad p
   - \dive\!\left(2\mu_f\bm D(\uv)\right) - \dive\sig_{\!s}(\xiv,\psib) - \bm f = \bm 0,\\
R_{p} &= \dive\uv = 0,\\
\bm R_{\xiv} &= \frac{\xiv-\tilde{\xiv}}{\dt} + (\uv\!\cdot\!\grad)\xiv = \bm 0,\\
\bm R_{\psib} &= \frac{\psib-\tilde{\psib}}{\dt} - \bm g(\grad\uv,\psib;\tau) = \bm 0,
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\tilde{(\cdot)}$ denotes the semi-Lagrangian departure-point value and $\bm g$ is
the local log-conformation RHS \cref{eq:logconf-rhs} (with the relaxation handled by the
exact substep). \Cref{eq:residual} is solved by an inexact Newton--Krylov iteration
\cite{Knoll2004}; the Jacobian action uses the analytic material tangents
$\partial\sig_{\!s}/\partial\xiv$ and $\partial\sig_{\!s}/\partial\psib$ (the latter via
$\partial\exp\psib/\partial\psib$, closed-form in 2-D). The velocity--pressure block is a
saddle point preconditioned by the exact projection of \cref{sec:discretization} (the
DCT/FFT Poisson solve approximates the pressure Schur complement); the elastic block
adds a symmetric-positive stiffness to the velocity operator, improving conditioning.

\paragraph{Non-smooth operators.} A clean Newton linearization requires differentiable
$\bm R_{\xiv},\bm R_{\psib}$; the default semi-Lagrangian interpolation,
band extrapolation and level-set rebuild are only piecewise smooth. We (i) switch
$\xiv/\psib$ transport to the conservative flux form (differentiable Jacobian
$\partial\bm R_{\xiv}/\partial\uv$) and (ii) freeze the interface geometry
($\phi$, band, $H$, departure points) at the start of the step, updating it in an outer
fixed-point iteration---an ``implicit elastic kernel with lagged interface.''

\paragraph{What is realized here.} \Cref{eq:residual} is the target. We realize its
\emph{velocity--pressure} block as the semi-monolithic scheme (Algorithm~\ref{alg:mono}): a
JFNK solve of $\bm R_{\!\uv},R_p$ with advection implicit in the residual, preconditioned by
one IMEX step and the exact projection, with $(\xiv,\psib)$ held fixed within the Newton
iteration. This block is exercised on the fluid (\cref{sec:results-mono}); it is not yet run
as a coupled FSI. The coupled \emph{elastic} FSI results in this paper are instead produced
by the \textsc{IMEX-SL} scheme (Algorithm~\ref{alg:imexsl}) with the energy-conserving
trapezoidal elastic coupling of \cref{sec:elastic-stab} (the reference map transported by the
implicit-midpoint velocity), which lifts the viscous, elastic-wave and advective limits in a
fractional-step setting and delivers the net FSI speedup of \cref{sec:disc-speedup} at the
cost of an $O(\dt)$ operator-split error. Closing the outer iteration to a fully-consistent
Newton over all of $(\uv,p,\xiv,\psib)$---which \eqref{eq:residual} defines and the analytic
tangent above supports---is the natural next step and is left to future work. This differs
from the monolithic Eulerian FSI of \cite{Richter2013,Dunne2006}, which carries the solid
deformation \emph{inside} the Newton system and lags only the interface/domain-extension
step; the split here is coarser (the whole $(\xiv,\psib)$ is lagged), and removing it is
precisely what the fully-coupled solve above would achieve.

\subsection{The four time-integration schemes}
\label{sec:four-schemes}
We compare four schemes of increasing implicitness (\cref{alg:explicit,alg:imex,alg:imexsl,alg:mono});
each shares the exact projection and the semi-Lagrangian transport of $(\xiv,\psib)$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]\small\caption{\textsc{Explicit} --- one step}\label{alg:explicit}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State advect $(\xiv,\psib)$ with $\uv^n$ (semi-Lagrangian); extrapolate into the band
\State $\psib\!\leftarrow\!$ explicit relaxation; $\be\!=\!\exp\psib$; assemble blended stress $\sig$, force $\bm f=\dive\sig$
\State $\uv^{*}=\uv^n+\dt\!\left[-(\uv^n\!\cdot\!\grad)\uv^n+\nu\nabla^2\uv^n+\bm f/\rho\right]$
\State $(\uv^{n+1},p)\leftarrow\textsc{Project}(\uv^{*})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]\small\caption{\textsc{IMEX} --- implicit viscosity + exact relaxation}\label{alg:imex}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State advect $(\xiv,\psib)$; extrapolate; \, $\psib\!\leftarrow\!$ \emph{exact} exponential relaxation ($A$-stable $\forall\tau$)
\State $\be,\ \sig,\ \bm f$ as above
\State solve $\left(\It-\dt\,\nu\nabla^2\right)\uv^{*}=\uv^n-\dt(\uv^n\!\cdot\!\grad)\uv^n+\dt\,\bm f/\rho$ \Comment{FFT/DCT, or PCG at walls}
\State $(\uv^{n+1},p)\leftarrow\textsc{Project}(\uv^{*})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]\small\caption{\textsc{IMEX-SL} --- + semi-Lagrangian advection}\label{alg:imexsl}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State advect $(\xiv,\psib)$; extrapolate; exact relaxation; \, $\be,\sig,\bm f$
\If{$\dt\,\max|\uv^n|/\dx \le 1$} $\tilde{\uv}\leftarrow \uv^n-\dt(\uv^n\!\cdot\!\grad)\uv^n$ \Comment{central (accurate)}
\Else\ \ $\tilde{\uv}\leftarrow$ semi-Lagrangian backtrace of $\uv^n$ (cubic) \Comment{unconditionally stable}
\EndIf
\State solve $\left(\It-\dt\,\nu\nabla^2\right)\uv^{*}=\tilde{\uv}+\dt\,\bm f/\rho$; \ $(\uv^{n+1},p)\leftarrow\textsc{Project}(\uv^{*})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]\small\caption{\textsc{Semi-monolithic} --- Newton--Krylov velocity--pressure}\label{alg:mono}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State advect $(\xiv,\psib)$ with $\uv^n$ (lagged); extrapolate; \, $\be,\sig,\bm f$
\State solve $\bm R(\uv,p)=\bm 0$ by JFNK, with
$\bm R_{\uv}=\tfrac{\uv-\uv^n}{\dt}+(\uv\!\cdot\!\grad)\uv+\tfrac1\rho\grad p-\nu\nabla^2\uv-\tfrac{\bm f}{\rho}$, $R_p=\dive\uv$:
\State \quad Newton: $\bm J\,\delta=-\bm R$ via GMRES, preconditioned by one \textsc{IMEX} step + the projection Schur complement
\State $(\uv^{n+1},p)\leftarrow$ converged state
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\Cref{tab:schemes} summarizes them. \textsc{Explicit} and \textsc{IMEX} treat advection
explicitly (central), so both carry the advective CFL; they differ only in the viscous
(and relaxation) limits, and on non-stiff-advection problems give identical accuracy---so
in the results we report \textsc{IMEX} as the representative implicit-viscosity scheme and
do not separately plot \textsc{Explicit} where the two coincide. \textsc{IMEX-SL} lifts the
advective limit; \textsc{Semi-monolithic} additionally makes advection and the
velocity--pressure coupling implicit and consistent (no split/SL error), monolithic in
$(\uv,p)$ with $(\xiv,\psib)$ lagged---the fully-coupled $(\uv,p,\xiv,\psib)$ Newton is
\cref{sec:monolithic}.

\section{Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

\subsection{Constitutive update}
The log-conformation update is verified against the analytic viscometric limits of
\cref{sec:constitutive}. Step-strain relaxation, steady simple shear (including the
first normal-stress difference $N_1$), and the elastic limit $\tau\to\infty$ are all
reproduced to machine precision:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
test & analytic target & error \\
\midrule
step-strain relaxation & $\sigma_{xy}=G\gamma e^{-t/\tau}$ & $\sim10^{-10}$ \\
steady shear & $\sigma_{xy}=G\tau\dot\gamma,\ N_1=2G\tau^2\dot\gamma^2$ & $\sim10^{-12}$ \\
elastic limit & $\be=\Ften\Ften^{\!\top}$ & $\sim10^{-13}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
With the exact relaxation substep \cref{eq:relax-exact} the step-strain response is
reproduced to machine precision at \emph{any} $\dt$, whereas the explicit update errs by
$0.29$ at $\dt/\tau=2$ and diverges by $\dt/\tau=5$. Positive-definiteness of $\be$ is
retained under extreme shear.

\subsection{Operators and projection}
The MAC divergence is exact on linear fields and second-order on smooth fields; the
identity $\mathrm{div}\circ\mathrm{grad}\equiv\mathrm{Lap}$ holds to $10^{-9}$; the
Poisson round-trip recovers the field to $10^{-10}$; and projecting an arbitrary
wall-boundary velocity yields divergence below $10^{-11}$ (machine-zero, the property the
collocated solver cannot reach). The FFT Helmholtz solve of \cref{eq:helmholtz} passes
the same round-trip to $10^{-10}$.

\subsection{Integrator equivalence and large-step stability}
On the periodic Taylor--Green decaying vortex the explicit, IMEX and semi-monolithic
integrators produce \emph{identical} errors at matched $\dt$: the implicit and
semi-Lagrangian machinery does not perturb the solution it is meant to accelerate. (For
non-stiff advection the adaptive semi-monolithic scheme reduces to the central-difference
IMEX, \cref{sec:monolithic}, so the three coincide.) The implicit-viscosity scheme is
additionally stable and accurate up to $\dt=50\times$ the explicit viscous limit
$\dx^2/(4\nu)$, where the explicit scheme diverges. The spatial order of the coupled
method is measured directly on the FSI benchmark in \cref{sec:disc-tg}, which is the
honest test for a one-fluid interface.

\subsection{Energy conservation of the elastic coupling}
On the linear elastic standing shear wave the trapezoidal coupling
(Proposition~\ref{prop:trap}) is genuinely lossless. Measured with the discrete energy
invariant tied to the \emph{same} Laplacian used in the update, the drift is at round-off
($\le2\times10^{-14}$) for every step from $0.5\times$ to $8\times$ the explicit CFL,
confirming $|\lambda|=1$ (top row); the dissipative stage-1 stabilizer instead damps the
wave away (drift $\to1$) and the explicit scheme diverges above the CFL.

\begin{tabular}{@{}lccccc@{}}
\toprule
$\dt/\dt_{\rm CFL}$ & $0.5$ & $1$ & $2$ & $4$ & $8$ \\
\midrule
consistent-energy drift & $1.9\!\times\!10^{-14}$ & $6.9\!\times\!10^{-15}$ &
$1.5\!\times\!10^{-14}$ & $9.5\!\times\!10^{-15}$ & $1.8\!\times\!10^{-14}$ \\
centered-difference drift & $5.5\!\times\!10^{-5}$ & $2.2\!\times\!10^{-4}$ &

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The apparent growth in the second row---an earlier measure that computes the elastic

solver reproduces the analytic $\det\be=1+\mathrm{Wi}^2$ (e.g.\ $1.36$ at
$\mathrm{Wi}=0.6$), a bounded, physical departure whose trace is carried by the pressure.

\subsection{Neo-Hookean (elastic) disc in a Taylor--Green vortex: FSI convergence}
\label{sec:disc-tg}
The first FSI test measures the spatial order of the coupled discretization. A
neo-Hookean \emph{elastic} disc is carried by a decaying Taylor--Green vortex---a smooth,
closed-streamline flow with no walls---so that the only error source is the one-fluid
interface coupling itself. Measuring the L2 error against the finest grid ($N{=}128$) over
$N=32$--$128$ (\cref{fig:disc-tg-conv}) gives velocity $\approx1.8$ and kinetic/total
energy $\approx2$, but \emph{pressure only $\approx1$}: the diffuse interface caps the
pressure (and, more mildly, the velocity) below the second order the same solver attains
on a wall-free single-mode fluid flow, consistent with Jain \emph{et al.}\
\cite{Jain2019}. The reduced order is intrinsic to the smoothed one-fluid interface, not
to the time integrator---the explicit, IMEX and semi-monolithic schemes share the spatial
discretization and coincide (\cref{sec:verification}); a sharp-interface treatment
\cite{Kolahdouz2023} is the route to higher field order and is a limitation of the present
method.

\subsection{Neo-Hookean (elastic) disc in a lid-driven cavity (FSI)}

\emph{et al.}\ \cite{Jain2019}.
The centroid trajectory follows the reference over the full inward spiral with a mean
deviation of $1.4\times10^{-2}$ at $N{=}64$ and $1.3\times10^{-2}$ at $N{=}128$
(max $\approx4\times10^{-2}$)---about $3.5\%$ of the trajectory extent. The deviation is
essentially grid-independent ($N{=}64\approx N{=}128$), so the residual gap to Sugiyama's
$1024^2$ result is a genuine diffuse-interface/model difference (\cref{sec:disc-tg}), not
under-resolution---consistent with the finite-difference RMT of Jain \emph{et al.}\
\cite{Jain2019}, which reports a comparable diffuse-interface offset from the same benchmark. The IMEX integrator reproduces the explicit
(validated) centroid trajectory to $2\times10^{-3}$ with identical minimum Jacobian,
confirming that the implicit viscosity does not perturb the validated FSI result
(\cref{fig:disc-centroid}, \cref{tab:disc-integrators}). Over the full inward spiral the
explicit, IMEX and semi-Lagrangian (IMEX-SL) integrators trace the same centroid
path---with IMEX-SL completing it in a quarter of the steps (\cref{sec:disc-speedup}).

\subsection{Viscoelastic constitutive response}
\label{sec:ve-fsi}
The finite-strain viscoelastic branch is validated against closed-form rheometric
solutions of the upper-convected Maxwell (UCM) model, exercising the log-conformation
update ($\psib=\log\be$, $\sig_{\!s}=G\be$) across transient, frequency-domain and
high-Weissenberg regimes (\cref{fig:ve-valid}; $G=\tau=1$ so $\mathrm{Wi}$ reads directly
off the imposed rate).
\emph{Startup of steady shear} (\cref{fig:ve-valid}a) reproduces the transient shear stress
$\sigma_{xy}(t)=G\tau\dot\gamma\,(1-e^{-t/\tau})$ and first normal-stress difference
$N_1(t)=2G\tau^2\dot\gamma^2\,[\,1-(1+t/\tau)e^{-t/\tau}]$ to a maximum error of
$1.8\times10^{-6}$.
\emph{Small-amplitude oscillatory shear} (\cref{fig:ve-valid}b) recovers the Maxwell
storage and loss moduli $G'(\omega)=G(\omega\tau)^2/[1+(\omega\tau)^2]$ and
$G''(\omega)=G\omega\tau/[1+(\omega\tau)^2]$---including the crossover at $\omega\tau=1$
and the elastic plateau $G'\!\to\!G$---to $2\times10^{-4}$ over three decades of frequency.
\emph{Steady planar extension} (\cref{fig:ve-valid}c) reproduces the analytic elastic
eigenvalues $b_e^{xx}=1/(1-2\mathrm{Wi})$ and $b_e^{yy}=1/(1+2\mathrm{Wi})$, tracking the
coil--stretch growth of $b_e^{xx}$ up to $\mathrm{Wi}\!\to\!\tfrac12$ (relative error below
$3\%$, set by the finite integration time near the singularity) while $\be$ remains
symmetric positive-definite throughout---the property the matrix-logarithm transport
guarantees and a direct $\be$ update loses. The two-way-coupled behaviour is the subject of
\cref{sec:ve-coupled}.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/viscoelastic_validation.png}
\caption{Viscoelastic constitutive validation of the log-conformation update against
analytic UCM/Maxwell solutions ($G=\tau=1$): (a) startup of steady shear at
$\mathrm{Wi}=1$---transient $\sigma_{xy}$ and $N_1$ (markers) vs analytic (grey);
(b) SAOS storage/loss moduli $G',G''$ vs Maxwell over three decades of $\omega\tau$;
(c) steady planar-extension eigenvalues of $\be$ vs $1/(1\mp2\mathrm{Wi})$, showing the

\label{fig:ve-valid}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Two-way-coupled viscoelastic FSI: an SLS disc in a Taylor--Green vortex}
\label{sec:ve-coupled}
We now couple the full \emph{standard-linear-solid} of \cref{sec:constitutive} to the flow:
the disc of \cref{sec:disc-tg} carries an equilibrium neo-Hookean branch
$\mu_s\dev(\Ften\Ften^{\!\top})$ (from the reference map) \emph{and} a relaxing Maxwell
branch $G\dev\be$ (from the transported $\psib=\log\be$), with $\mu_s=G=0.1$, in the same
wall-free Taylor--Green vortex (\cref{fig:ve-coupled}). Four results establish the coupling.

\emph{(a) Convergence.} At fixed $\tau=1$ the SLS FSI fields converge under refinement with
the same interface-limited orders as the elastic case---velocity near second order, kinetic
energy second order, pressure first order (\cref{fig:ve-coupled}a)---so the added transport
does not degrade the discretization.

\emph{(b) Relaxation vs.\ shape memory.} The stored elastic energy (\cref{fig:ve-coupled}b)
shows the defining SLS behaviour: shortening $\tau$ dissipates the relaxing branch, but the
non-relaxing equilibrium branch keeps the body returning toward its shape---the energy does
not decay to zero as it would for a Maxwell \emph{fluid}.

\emph{(c) Robustness.} $\be$ stays symmetric positive-definite throughout: $\det\be$ is
bounded near unity, rising mildly with the local Weissenberg number toward the analytic
$1+\mathrm{Wi}^2$ (\cref{fig:ve-coupled}c, left), and the solid area is conserved to within
$1$--$2\%$ (right), so the captured interface stays intact over the run.

\emph{(d) The $\tau\to\infty$ limit and the two strain descriptions.} A subtlety raised by
using both $\be$ and $\Ften=(\grad\xiv)^{-1}$: at $\tau\to\infty$ (pure elastic) the
transported $\be$ and the reference-map $\Ften\Ften^{\!\top}$ are the \emph{same} continuum
tensor, but they are advanced by two different discrete transports, so they drift apart under
mesh refinement rather than agreeing exactly. \Cref{fig:ve-coupled}d plots
$\|\exp\psib-\Ften\Ften^{\!\top}\|/\|\Ften\Ften^{\!\top}\|$ over the regularly-deformed
solid: it grows from zero to ${\approx}0.4$ by the end of the run, a bilinear
semi-Lagrangian transport error (reducible with a less-diffusive advection or finer grid).
Crucially the \emph{dynamics} are insensitive to it---the $\tau\to\infty$ Maxwell run
reproduces the reference-map neo-Hookean kinetic-energy history to
$\max|\Delta\mathrm{KE}|/\mathrm{KE}=3.8\times10^{-4}$---because the elastic stress is a small
perturbation on the flow, and in the SLS model the shape-memory (equilibrium) branch is built
from $\grad\xiv$ directly, so this drift affects only the relaxing branch. We therefore claim
recovery of the elastic \emph{dynamics}, not of the strain field pointwise.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/viscoelastic_disc_tg.png}
\caption{Two-way-coupled standard-linear-solid disc in a Taylor--Green vortex
($\mu_s=G=0.1$): (a) FSI mesh convergence at $\tau=1$ (interface-limited, as in the elastic
case); (b) stored elastic energy vs.\ time for $\tau=\infty,1,0.25$ with an elastic-only
baseline---relaxation dissipates the relaxing branch while the equilibrium branch retains
shape; (c) $\max\det\be$ (solid, left) bounded near unity and solid area (dashed, right)
conserved to $1$--$2\%$; (d) drift between the two strain descriptions
$\|\exp\psib-\Ften\Ften^{\!\top}\|/\|\Ften\Ften^{\!\top}\|$ at $\tau=\infty$, a transport
error to which the dynamics are insensitive ($\Delta\mathrm{KE}/\mathrm{KE}=3.8\times10^{-4}$).}
\label{fig:ve-coupled}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Lifted time-step limits}
Each level of the IMEX stack is verified to reproduce the explicit result at a matched
step before its lifted step is exercised (\cref{tab:cfl-lift}). Implicit viscosity is
stable to $50\times$ the explicit viscous CFL on Taylor--Green; the combined IMEX
reproduces the four-roll extensional blob $b_e^{xx}$ to $0.55\%$ at matched $\dt$ and
completes within ${\sim}2\%$ at $2$--$4\times$ the explicit viscous CFL where the
explicit scheme diverges; the linearly-implicit elastic stabilizer is consistent
($0.44\%$ vs.\ explicit at $0.1\times$ the elastic CFL) and remains stable at
$8\times$ the elastic CFL, where the explicit elastic force diverges; and the
semi-Lagrangian advection (\cref{sec:monolithic}) removes the final advective limit.
Crucially, the wall Helmholtz is solved by a DST-preconditioned CG that converges in a
handful of iterations independent of resolution ($17\!\to\!5$ at $N{=}128$), so the
lifted $\dt$ translates into a wall-clock gain rather than being consumed by the solve.

\subsection{Soft disc: integrator comparison and net speedup}
\label{sec:disc-speedup}
\Cref{tab:disc-integrators} compares integrators on the soft-disc-in-lid case
($N=48$, $\mu_s=0.1$). The case is \emph{advection}-limited: implicit viscosity alone
(\texttt{imex}) reproduces the trajectory at the matched step ($\Delta$centroid
$2.2\times10^{-3}$) but cannot enlarge $\dt$, so it yields no speedup---confirming that
the last binding constraint is advection, not diffusion. Adding semi-Lagrangian
advection (\texttt{imex-sl}) lifts it: at $6\times$ the explicit $\dt$ the run completes

trajectory within ${\sim}6\%$ of the explicit reference (the residual is the
semi-Lagrangian numerical diffusion, which vanishes as $\dt$ is reduced and would be
removed by the consistent-tangent variant of \cref{sec:monolithic}).

\paragraph{Work--precision and the regime where implicit pays off.}
\Cref{fig:work-precision} plots accuracy against wall-clock for the three FSI-applicable
schemes at $N=64$ and $N=128$; the error is the max centroid deviation from a common fine
reference (explicit at $N{=}128$ and its natural step). The $N{=}64$ explicit point already
sits at ${\approx}2\times10^{-3}$---the spatial-discretization floor at that resolution---so
the \texttt{imex-sl} points at $2$--$4\times$ the step lie \emph{at} that floor (their
temporal error is below the spatial error), which is the favourable outcome. Two things are
visible. At $N=64$ the semi-Lagrangian \texttt{imex-sl} scheme is Pareto-optimal: taking
$6$--$8\times$ the explicit step it reaches the same accuracy band in $2.5$--$3.7\times$ less
wall-clock, because it lifts the binding \emph{advective} limit at essentially the explicit
per-step cost. Implicit viscosity alone (\texttt{imex}) does
\emph{not} help here---advection, not diffusion, binds---and its iterative solve makes it
slower. At $N=128$, where the viscous limit binds the explicit step, the picture is
governed by the cost of the wall-bounded Helmholtz solve: the implicit step is
$\sim\!2\times$ larger but each step costs several times more, so implicit integration only
wins once the lifted-to-explicit step ratio exceeds that per-step overhead---the low-Reynolds,
stiff-solid, or finer-grid regime. The comparison thus \emph{delineates} rather than
universally asserts the benefit: the semi-Lagrangian step pays off broadly, while the
implicit viscous/elastic solves pay off specifically where the explicit step is throttled by
more than the solve cost.

\subsection{Stiff-solid regime: enabling stability}
\label{sec:stiff}
The introduction motivates implicit integration partly by the stiff-solid regime; we
exercise it directly as an FSI case. A neo-Hookean disc with $\mu_s=16$
($c_s=\sqrt{\mu_s/\rho}=4\,U_{\rm lid}$) makes the elastic wave the binding explicit limit.
\Cref{fig:stiff}a shows the outcome: the explicit scheme diverges once $\dt$ exceeds its
combined elastic/advective CFL (blow-up at $6\times$ the elastic CFL, $\min J$ collapsing at
$t\!\approx\!0.07$), whereas the energy-conserving trapezoidal coupling
(Proposition~\ref{prop:trap}) advances the same disc stably at $4\times$ and $8\times$ that
step, the minimum Jacobian staying near $0.9$ so the body remains intact. This unconditional
stability is what makes the stiff regime reachable at all. Accuracy at these large steps is,
however, only $O(\dt)$ (\cref{fig:temporal}): the trajectory (\cref{fig:stiff}b) carries a
visible operator-split error, so at \emph{matched accuracy} the stiff case obeys the same
step-size-versus-solve-cost trade as \cref{sec:disc-speedup}, and the fully-consistent Newton
of \cref{sec:monolithic} is the route to large-step accuracy. The relaxation-stiff
counterpart---advancing a small-$\tau$ solid at $\dt\!\gg\!\tau$---is handled by the exact
substep, verified to machine precision at any $\dt$ in \cref{sec:verification}, and the
finite-$\tau$ coupled behaviour is the SLS case of \cref{sec:ve-coupled}.

\subsection{Semi-monolithic velocity--pressure solver (fluid)}
\label{sec:results-mono}
The four schemes above lift the FSI step-size limits by fractional-step means: the
\textsc{IMEX-SL} runs of \cref{sec:disc-speedup} use the energy-conserving trapezoidal
elastic coupling (lossless on the standing wave, \cref{sec:verification}) with
semi-Lagrangian advection, at the price of an $O(\dt)$ operator-split/semi-Lagrangian error.
The semi-monolithic scheme (\cref{alg:mono}) removes that error by making advection implicit
and \emph{consistent}: it solves the $(\uv,p)$ system by JFNK, preconditioned by one IMEX
step and the exact projection. We demonstrate it here on the fluid. On Taylor--Green it
reproduces the analytic decay ($1.5\times10^{-4}$ at $\dt$ below the CFL) and, crucially,
stays accurate far above the advective CFL: at $5\times$ the CFL its error is
$1.1\times10^{-3}$ (clean $O(\dt)$), an order of magnitude below the $2.7\times10^{-2}$
semi-Lagrangian diffusion floor at the same step, with Newton converging in ${\sim}3$
iterations per step. Extending this velocity--pressure solve to the coupled solid---the full
Newton of \cref{sec:monolithic}---is future work; the coupled results in this paper use the
IMEX-SL scheme.

\section{Conclusions and outlook}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented an incompressible, one-fluid Reference Map Technique for viscoelastic
fluid--structure interaction on a single staggered grid. The stress-carrying elastic
strain is transported as a log-conformation tensor, guaranteeing positive-definiteness and
decoupling the relaxing stress from the folding-prone reference map, and the staggered
discretization makes the projection exact. On this spatial method we built four
time-integration schemes of increasing implicitness: an implicit--explicit stack that
removes the viscous, relaxation and elastic-wave step-size limits in turn---reusing the
pressure transform/Krylov solvers, with a trapezoidal elastic coupling that conserves the
discrete energy to round-off---and a semi-monolithic Newton--Krylov scheme that advances
the velocity--pressure system implicitly with the reference map and conformation lagged.
A work--precision comparison delineates where this pays off: the semi-Lagrangian step lowers
cost at controlled accuracy broadly, while the implicit viscous/elastic solves win once the
lifted step exceeds their per-step cost (low Reynolds number, stiff solids, or fine grids).
We demonstrated the coupled
viscoelastic response on a disc in a Taylor--Green vortex: $\tau\to\infty$ recovers the
elastic dynamics, finite $\tau$ dissipates stored elastic energy, and the elastic Finger
tensor remains positive-definite throughout.

Relative to the closest prior work, the method differs from explicit hyperelastic RMT
\cite{Kamrin2012,Rycroft2020,Jain2019} in both the viscoelastic constitutive transport
and the implicit time integration, from conformation-tensor viscoelasticity in that the
carrier is a \emph{solid} with an equilibrium branch inside an FSI solver, and from the
GPR unified model \cite{Peshkov2016,Dumbser2016} in being incompressible and implicit
rather than compressible and explicit/semi-implicit.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The formulation is two-dimensional; density contrast is limited
to the variable-coefficient projection path; the diffuse-interface FSI fields converge
below second order; and near-contact resolution is penalty-based. The linearly-implicit
elastic stabilizer (stage~1) adds numerical damping at large $\dt$, which the
energy-conserving trapezoidal coupling (\cref{sec:elastic-stab}) removes; the coupled runs
use the latter.

\paragraph{Outlook.} Natural extensions are three dimensions, variable density and true
sedimentation, lubrication-corrected contact \cite{Fai2018}, multi-mode/nonlinear
(Giesekus, FENE-P) constitutive laws, and---most directly---closing the outer iteration to
the fully-coupled $(\uv,p,\xiv,\psib)$ Newton posed in \cref{sec:monolithic}.
