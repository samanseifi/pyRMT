\title{A semi-implicit log-conformation Reference Map Technique for incompressible
viscoelastic fluid--structure interaction}

\author[bu]{Saman Seifi\corref{cor}}\ead{samansei@bu.edu}
\cortext[cor]{Corresponding author}
\address[bu]{Department of Mechanical Engineering, Boston University,
Boston, MA 02215, USA}

\begin{abstract}
We present a fully Eulerian, one-fluid Reference Map Technique (RMT) for incompressible
fluid--structure interaction (FSI) that couples a finite-strain \emph{viscoelastic} solid
to a Newtonian fluid on a single staggered (MAC) grid with one pressure solve. The
stress-carrying elastic strain is transported as a log-conformation tensor
$\psib=\log\be$, positive-definite by construction, which decouples the relaxing elastic
stress from the folding-prone reference map; as the relaxation time $\tau\to\infty$ the
model reduces to the neo-Hookean RMT. On this spatial method we build a family of four
time-integration schemes of increasing implicitness---explicit; an implicit--explicit
(IMEX) scheme with backward-Euler viscosity through the pressure transforms and an exact
exponential relaxation; a semi-Lagrangian variant (IMEX-SL); and a semi-monolithic
Newton--Krylov (JFNK) solve of the velocity--pressure system---which remove the viscous,
relaxation, elastic-wave and advective step-size limits in turn, each verified to reproduce
the explicit result before its target limit is lifted. The semi-monolithic scheme advances
$(\uv,p)$ implicitly with the reference map and conformation $(\xiv,\psib)$ lagged in an
outer iteration, as in existing monolithic Eulerian FSI; the fully-coupled Newton over
$(\uv,p,\xiv,\psib)$ is posed and left as the next step. The staggered discretization makes
the projection exact (machine-zero divergence), and the trapezoidal elastic coupling
conserves the discrete energy to round-off at every step. We validate the constitutive
update against analytic Maxwell solutions (startup of shear, oscillatory moduli, planar
extension), the fluid and neo-Hookean-solid FSI against the lid-driven cavity and a
disc-in-vortex convergence study, and---the point of the title---a two-way-coupled
\emph{viscoelastic} disc in a Taylor--Green vortex, where $\tau\to\infty$ recovers the
elastic dynamics, finite $\tau$ dissipates stored elastic energy, and $\be$ stays
positive-definite. A work--precision study delineates where the implicit stack pays off: the
semi-Lagrangian scheme reaches a target accuracy at lower wall-clock by taking several-fold
larger steps, while the benefit of the implicit viscous/elastic solves is set by how far the
step can grow relative to the per-step solve cost. Each capability is reported at its true
status.
\end{abstract}
